2. Origin Story & Empirical Calibration: Why Naive Swing Trading Fails

2.1 The CROPSTER Incident: The Stop-Loss Fallacy

In early testing, the system observed micro-caps like CROPSTER locked at 5% daily Lower Circuit (LC):

  • Naive belief: An algorithm sets a "2% stop loss" and assumes an exit occurs when price drops 2%.
  • Market reality: On an illiquid micro-cap locked at Lower Circuit, the order book displays Total Bids = 0. Sell orders queue behind tens of thousands of trapped shares.
  • The Result: The stop loss cannot execute. The stock drops 5% every day for 10 straight sessions:
Drawdown Factor = 1 - (1 - 0.05)10 = 1 - (0.95)10 = 1 - 0.59874 = 40.126% ≈ -40.1%

Architectural Lesson: Stop losses do not exist when bid depth is zero. Sizing must be bounded by worst-case unbroken circuit descent, not arbitrary stop distances.

2.2 The CHANDRIMA Incident: The Market Participation Trap

On 10 September 2024, an analysis of CHANDRIMA revealed a proposed entry of 4,500 shares against a total daily turnover of 6,355 shares:

  • The Trap: 4,500 shares represented 70.8% of the entire day's market volume.
  • The Consequence: At 70.8% participation, the trader is not trading into the market; the trader is the market. Exiting would take over 10 trading sessions, creating a fatal adverse-selection trap where fills occur only when an institutional operator dumps into retail ("buying the exit").

- **Arbitrage Defined:** Arbitrage requires a riskless, simultaneous transaction that extracts profit from a pricing inefficiency.

  $$\Delta \text{FX Rate} \approx \text{Inflation}_{\text{Domestic}} - \text{Inflation}_{\text{Foreign}}$$
  Because India's structural inflation has historically averaged 5%–7% compared to the US's 2%–3%, the rupee naturally depreciates by the ~3%–4% difference to keep trade competitive.
- **Compounding Comparison (Nominal vs. Real Returns):**
  - Indian corporate earnings (Nifty 50) compound at 12%–14% CAGR in INR because nominal Indian GDP growth contains domestic inflation.
  - In USD terms, Nifty compounds at ~8.5%–10.5% CAGR.
  - The S&P 500 historically compounds at ~9.5%–10.5% CAGR in USD.
  - Converting S&P 500 returns to INR:
    $$\text{Total Return (INR)} = (1 + \text{US Equity Return}) \times (1 + \Delta \text{USD/INR}) - 1 \approx (1.095) \times (1.035) - 1 = \mathbf{13.3\% \text{ CAGR}}$$
  - **Verdict:** An Indian investor in the S&P 500 earns ~13.3% CAGR in INR, which is essentially identical to holding the domestic Nifty 50. Currency depreciation does not create "free money."

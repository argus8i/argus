Priority findings

“High” means the issue can materially change eligibility, execution, risk or reported performance. Locations refer to the inspected source snapshot; proposed fixes have not been applied.

01 · The circuit engine crashes on both locked-circuit branches

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

circuit_rules.py:97 calls removed enum member EMERGENCY_AMO_EXIT; line 105 calls removed BUY_AMO_ENTRY. Both produced AttributeError. The script’s own demonstration also stops at the lower-circuit branch.

Impact: the engine fails precisely when execution risk matters most. Merely restoring the old names would reintroduce a prohibited locked-upper-circuit buy branch.

Required: reconcile the branch semantics with the current rules, then regression-test every signal path, including exclusions before any signal generation.

02 · The eight rules are documentation, not a unified enforced gate

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: all three reviewers

At circuit_rules.py:88, surveillance filtering omits ESM Stage 1, GSM Stage 1 and ASM. Synthetic ESM_1, ASM_1 and sub-₹10 inputs each returned HOLD instead of disqualification/freeze. Unknown classification defaults to EQ/NONE. The accumulation-buy enum is defined but not returned. No code enforces the 60-session/20-trade/positive-net-expectancy milestone.

Required: one versioned eligibility decision used by every component; unknown or stale required fields must block qualification. Keep surveillance freeze/exit review distinct from an executable sell.

03 · A 20% spread qualifies despite the <1% rule

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

accumulation_screener.py:22 silently defaults missing spread to 0.5%. Line 122 reports spread, but no eligibility condition checks it. Replacing every spread in a qualifying synthetic history with 20% still returned QUALIFIED.

Required: explicit spread validation and rejection at the threshold. Historical daily candles without contemporaneous bid/offer data cannot establish that an entry met a live spread condition.

04 · Missing numerical data and duplicate sessions pass the screen

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

At accumulation_screener.py:52–122, NaN volume and NaN delivery each bypass comparisons and yield QUALIFIED. A history of 85 rows all bearing the same date also qualifies. Dates, finite values, OHLC consistency and units lack adequate validation.

Required: validate unique chronological exchange sessions, finite positive prices, nonnegative volumes, fraction-versus-percent units and required fields before computing features. The missing Tuple import additionally breaks annotation resolution in Python 3.14; current lazy annotation handling hides the issue during ordinary execution.

05 · Position sizing does not enforce the loss budget

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

risk_calculator.py:11–15 declares a 2% portfolio loss budget, but calculate_position_size at lines 22–47 never uses it. It only caps allocation and a percentage of historical daily volume.

Illustrative audit sensitivity: ₹100,000 capital, ₹100 price and ample volume produce a ₹10,000 position. Ten consecutive 5% declines imply ₹4,012.63 loss before costs, exceeding the declared ₹2,000 risk budget. This is a synthetic control test, not a proposed position.

The trap function defaults to four days, not ten. The comment asserting that 1% of daily volume ensures an instant exit is unsupported. Historical turnover is not executable future bid depth.

Required: make loss budget and stress assumptions explicit inputs and enforce the tightest applicable constraint. Treat ten sessions as a minimum project stress, not a maximum possible loss. The observed screenshot set does not prove a complete ten-session, zero-bid lockout.

06 · The exit detector promises liquidity when there are no bids

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

A synthetic Day 4 price increase from ₹10 to ₹12, with zero bids and no upper-circuit flag, returned EXIT_INTO_BUY_QUEUE and “100% liquidity.” The target branch at volume_climax_detector.py:54–65 never validates the claimed exit market.

Line 33 assumes the first session’s close is the entry price. A lower-circuit flag with positive bids is also labelled a locked trap. Separately, circuit_rules.py:119 announces approximately 21.5% profit from the rally’s day count without knowing the position’s entry price.

Required: separate target detection, eligibility, executable orders and realised fills. Use actual paper-entry timestamps/quantities/prices; distinguish a price touching its limit from a no-counterparty lock.

07 · The four-state execution model has not been implemented

HIGH · Missing decision output · Owner: Antigravity + Claude

circuit_rules.py:27–31 defines four enum labels, but there is no order lifecycle consuming them. Neither this engine nor the simulator tracks price-specific queue position, executed opposing flow, partial quantities, cancellations, rejected orders or sale eligibility.

Whole-day cumulative volume cannot establish a fill: it may predate submission or trade at other prices. A zero resting bid is evidence of no immediately visible marketable exit, not proof that no incoming buyer will ever transact. A screenshot cannot establish a day-long lock.

Required: implement event-time, side-aware execution with explicit rejected/unknown states alongside the mandated lifecycle. Match only eligible volume after order submission at executable prices. Preserve uncertainty when depth or queue data is insufficient.

08 · Simulation probabilities are assumptions, not measured estimates

HIGH · Unsupported assumption · Owner: Claude

fill_model.py:37–50 hardcodes fill probabilities of 2%, 35% and 95%, plus transition probabilities. Line 143 assumes a 72% down-break probability; line 147 assigns an up-break return of 2%. No calibration sample, estimator, uncertainty intervals or holdout dataset supports these values.

Changing the lower-circuit range does not calibrate the other inputs. Running 200,000 paths reduces Monte Carlo noise around chosen assumptions; it does not create 200,000 observations.

Impact: published monthly-loss percentages and target-hit probabilities are scenario outputs, not established strategy statistics. Their negative direction also remains unproven.

Required: relabel current outputs as hypothetical; estimate parameters from properly timestamped, representative observations and test sensitivity before considering any empirical conclusion.

09 · The simulator’s entry, exit and monthly calendar are inconsistent

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Claude

fill_model.py:114–130 advances all paths through the full queue window, including paths already filled, then starts the holding-return calculation. Interim post-fill returns are omitted. Lines 158–160 return a net result even for positions without an exit event.

Illustrative audit sensitivity: temporarily forcing full entry fills and continued strong states in memory produced +20.092% “net” after four days, with no exit-order or bid checks. This reproduces the missing exit-accounting issue; it is not a forecast.

Down-breaks apply a sampled lower-circuit chain and immediately mark the trade done without consuming those sessions in a cash ledger. Lines 197–204 fit five “non-overlapping” attempts into 20 sessions although each uses three queue sessions plus four holding sessions—35 sessions before any lockout extension.

Required: one exchange-session calendar and cash/inventory ledger. Separate realised proceeds, marked open positions, unavailable cash and unresolved exits. A common simulation horizon must not count locked holdings as sale proceeds.

10 · The band monitor can treat invalid data as normal

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: Antigravity

band_revision_monitor.py:29–37 silently converts corrupt history into an empty history. Supplying previous close 100 and upper price 0 returned “Normal band at -100%.” A genuine narrowing alert disappeared on a repeated same-day check because the latest appended record became the new comparison baseline. A change only to the lower bound was ignored.

The module receives data; it does not itself fetch daily exchange files, monitor ESM/ASM/GSM classifications or run a scheduler. No operational daily feed was found.

Required: invalid/stale-data failure states, previous-trading-session comparison, idempotent daily writes, persistent alerts, both bounds, classification/circular evidence and explicit source timestamps. Band arithmetic alone does not prove a surveillance intervention.

11 · The designated paper log is malformed and contains no actual observations

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: ChatGPT

CHATGPT/observation_log.csv contains a 35-column header and one 34-field row labelled EXAMPLE-DELETE. Fields shift: “observation_only” becomes order status and “no” becomes signal label. The count of non-example rows is zero.

The shared trade log contains retrospective reconstructions, not the required prospective paper-trade evidence. One outcome is unknown and the Chandrima realised-P&L interpretation is unsupported.

Required: correct the schema in a separately reviewed change, keep examples out of the production ledger, validate rows on append, and derive milestone counts from verified prospective records. I accept responsibility for the malformed ChatGPT template and the unsupported interpretation repeated in my earlier analysis.

12 · Data provenance and security classification are not reliable enough

HIGH · Source contradiction / missing source · Owner: ChatGPT + Antigravity

The saved watchlist still contains obsolete BUY_AMO_ENTRY signals for sub-₹10 stocks. Chandrima’s saved band conflicts with later shared surveillance notes. Claimed exchange API checks lack an archived response and full source-to-field trail in the inspected materials; that makes them unauditable here, not automatically false.

accumulation_screener.py:63 accepts only the string EQ. A raw BSE group A input is rejected without an exchange-specific classification map. TickerData has no explicit exchange field. Company-name references for CCDL are inconsistent across notes.

Required: exchange + scrip code/ISIN identity, dated classification mapping, corporate-action history, source/ingestion times and raw-file preservation. Retire superseded signal outputs through versioning rather than silently treating them as current.

13 · Synthetic demonstrations are being mistaken for historical validation

HIGH · Unsupported assumption · Owner: all three reviewers

The screener’s Chandrima example generates a constructed price path around ₹10–₹12.375 rather than replaying the cited historical ₹6.5–₹8 setup. Other example names fail largely because of the price floor. Placeholder dates are not a verified trading calendar. A known winner below ₹10 cannot be required to pass a strategy whose adopted floor is ₹10.

No representative eligible universe with failures, delistings, non-fills and surveillance changes was found. The ranking specification is not an implemented, validated ranking model.

Required: separate unit fixtures, excluded historical case studies and prospective evaluation. Pre-register the signal/exit policy; retain all rejected and unfilled candidates; use out-of-time evaluation and transaction-cost accounting.

14 · Conflicting rule versions prevent reproducibility

HIGH · Formula/control defect · Owner: all three reviewers

Documents use different targets and holding periods: 8–10% over three sessions, 15–20% on Days 3/4, and earlier ChatGPT 5%/10% alternatives. Risk templates use different stress fractions. Child instructions and some outputs demand an immediate exit where the master rule requires a freeze and exit review. A sub-₹10 “exception paper trade” in my CCDL note conflicts with the current absolute floor.

Required: select one controlling version through tri-agent review, label legacy scenarios as superseded/excluded, and stamp every observation with the actual model version. Sub-₹10 case studies must not count toward compliant prospective-trade milestones.

What this means for the CCDL debate

“Hold Thursday, exit Friday at an exact price” is not validated by these models. Settlement eligibility, exchange order acceptance, available counterparties and actual execution are different questions; none is adequately represented by this code. A one-share order—even if accepted—would not prove a full-position fill or which day maximises expected returns. No live test order was submitted in this audit.

A large displayed bid queue does not supply a measured continuation probability. The project has no empirical basis for calling Day 4 the psychological peak or for predicting an automatic gap-up. This audit does not certify account-specific settlement availability or a current stock-specific surveillance status.

Repair sequence before validation resumes

  1. Freeze the specification. All three reviewers agree on one policy version, exclusions, unknown-data handling, entry timestamp, exit definition and permitted research use. Existing live holdings, if any, are a separate risk matter—not validation evidence or permission to deploy more money.
  2. Repair deterministic controls. Enum crashes, spread enforcement, finite/date validation, risk-budget plumbing, band-history integrity and CSV schema. Convert the reproduced failures into automated assertions.
  3. Build the execution/cash ledger. Track order eligibility, quantity, price, submission time, queue assumptions, partial fills, cancellations/rejections, cash availability, inventory and unresolved exits. Never book a target touch as a realised sale.
  4. Establish the data trail. Ingest and archive dated exchange identity, bands, surveillance lists, corporate actions, OHLCV/delivery and sufficiently granular execution evidence. Refresh statuses without overwriting historical truth.
  5. Rebuild evaluation on time-correct data. Use realistic event-time fills, a common session calendar and costs; expose uncertainty, non-fills and open positions. Calibrate probabilities rather than choosing them to support either side of the debate.
  6. Complete the prospective gate. At least 60 documented sessions and 20 realistically fillable paper entries, with verified positive net expectancy under the agreed policy. These minima are necessary project conditions, not statistical proof by themselves. Review uncertainty, correlated trades and robustness before any later capital discussion.

Evidence still required

Actual contract notes and cost basis for claimed completed trades; dated raw exchange classification/band responses; a source-backed trading calendar and corporate-action adjustments; granular order/trade evidence; all candidate and rejection records; and a signed-off, versioned evaluation specification.

The first downside failure is execution liquidity, followed by multi-session capital lockup—not merely a one-day 5% price change. No valuation or market-implied fundamental thesis has been built; proposed upside here is a momentum/continuation hypothesis. Its profitability, optimal target and best entry/exit time remain unestablished.

Scope, reproducibility and limitations

Reviewed all six core Python modules, the master and child project rules, shared protocol/mechanics/watchlist/trade log/questions, all three agents’ model specifications and substantive analysis/progress notes, the saved watchlist, the observation CSV and the screenshot evidence package. Re-inspected the pivotal screenshots for the disputed figures. There are 20 original JPG screenshots; these are not a continuous market-data history.

Executed a separate audit runner with 31 targeted probes under Python 3.14.7 and NumPy 2.5.2. These include successful controls and deliberately invalid synthetic inputs; 31 is not a count of independent defects, passed tests or market observations. The runner prints observations rather than serving as a production pass/fail test suite.

Reproduce from the project root: python -B CHATGPT/audit_checks_2026_09_09.py. The monitor is tested with persistence disabled in memory; probability replacements are temporary, isolated sensitivities. The output confirms identical SHA-256 hashes for all six core files before and after the diagnostic run. No trading model, broker account, order or core rule was changed.

Confidence: high for reproduced software defects and arithmetic; limited for historical trade profitability; not assessable for predictive edge, current broker eligibility or optimal CCDL exit. No live broker verification or complete historical backtest was performed. Official-source checks do not certify every dated regulatory assertion elsewhere in the project.

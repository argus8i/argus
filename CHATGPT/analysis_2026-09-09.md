# Circuit-bound microcap screenshot review

**As of:** 2026-09-09 IST  
**Status:** Conditional risk screen; not implementation-ready  
**Evidence:** 20 user-supplied screenshots plus public SEBI investor material. Screenshots alone cannot establish manipulation, beneficial ownership, spoofing, or intent.

## Executive conclusion

The images do show a recurring structure: large one-sided queues, circuit-constrained prices, sudden volume expansion, gaps, and near-zero visible liquidity on the opposite side. That structure can produce several consecutive gains on paper, but it creates asymmetric execution risk: entry may be impossible during an upper circuit and exit may be impossible after the queue reverses. The CROPSTER screenshots demonstrate this directly.

The proposed monthly target of four 5% days is mathematically misleading. Four compounded +5% closes equal **+21.55% before charges**, not a guaranteed realizable return. A subsequent four-day -5% sequence leaves the account about **1.0% below its starting value** before costs. More importantly, circuit locks can prevent an investor from realizing either a stop-loss or a displayed profit.

## Evidence-based red flags

1. **One-sided liquidity:** zero bids or zero offers means the position may be non-executable at the displayed circuit price.
2. **Queue concentration:** a huge quantity controlled by relatively few orders can change abruptly; quantity is not commitment.
3. **Pre-open contamination:** crossed prices, zero average price, or timestamps before continuous trading should be classified separately.
4. **Gap and path risk:** stop orders do not guarantee an exit when no buyer exists.
5. **Selective-screenshot bias:** only memorable winners, losses, and queue extremes are shown; there is no complete candidate universe.
6. **Price-level illusion:** low rupee price does not make a stock cheap or limit percentage loss.
7. **Target fixation:** a 20% monthly goal can cause progressively larger bets after missed circuits or losses.

## Safe decision framework

This framework is for avoiding manipulation traps, not exploiting or joining them.

### Automatic no-trade conditions

- Any tip, coordinated message, paid group, promoter-linked claim, or request to recruit other buyers.
- Security is currently under exchange suspension or a surveillance stage whose settlement/trading restrictions are not understood.
- No verified current announcement/filing explains the price-volume change.
- Entry is possible only at/near an upper circuit with no meaningful offers.
- Displayed depth is dominated by a handful of orders or is observed only in pre-open.
- A normal-sized sale cannot be completed within a conservative fraction of genuine traded volume.
- Missing official OHLCV, corporate-action, shareholding, and surveillance data.

### Research gate before any personal decision

For each symbol, verify from official sources: exact security code/ISIN, board/series, circuit band, ASM/GSM/ESM status, suspension notices, last 12 months of OHLCV and trade counts, deliverable quantity, corporate announcements, results, auditor qualifications, promoter holding/pledge, preferential issues/warrants, splits/bonuses, and bulk/block deals. Record the source and timestamp.

### Exit/liquidity posture

- Do not rely on a conventional stop-loss as the primary protection; a stop cannot execute without a counterparty.
- Treat a newly one-sided sell queue, first failed circuit, unexplained high-volume reversal, adverse surveillance change, or contradictory filing as an immediate **risk-review/attempted-exit trigger**, not a guaranteed fill.
- Never average down solely because a lower circuit has repeated.
- Never increase exposure to recover a prior loss or to meet a monthly target.
- If a position cannot tolerate several consecutive locked lower circuits without threatening essential capital, the position is too large. Exact sizing requires the user's capital, loss budget, other holdings, and verified liquidity data; those inputs are not present here.

## Model design: what can and cannot be trained

The current 20 images are not enough to train a reliable predictive model. A defensible study needs a prospective dataset with every candidate day, including non-events and failures. The unit of analysis should be one symbol-day, not one screenshot.

Candidate features: prior circuit streak, gap, intraday range, volume/20-day median volume, trade count, deliverable ratio, top-five bid/offer imbalance, number of orders behind the queue, queue persistence measured repeatedly through the day, market cap/free float, promoter holding/pledge, corporate-action flags, announcement flag, surveillance stage, and days since first abnormal move.

Labels: next-day openability, next-day return, maximum adverse/favorable excursion, number of locked lower-circuit days, whether a sale of a predefined small notional could execute, and time-to-exit.

Validation must be walk-forward by date, include fees/slippage/failed fills, retain delisted/suspended names, and freeze rules before observing outcomes. The key metric should be **realizable return and probability/time of exit**, not close-to-close return.

## User-reported third-cycle exit: correct interpretation

The user reports two earlier cycles with approximately 20% gains. In the third cycle, an intended exit was delayed; a later sell order was placed around 09:50 and a roughly ₹60,000 holding reportedly completed after approximately one hour, despite a displayed queue of roughly 40–60 lakh shares.

This is valuable as one execution observation, but it does not establish a 5–10% maximum loss. Under price-time priority, an order at the same circuit price generally executes after sell orders already ahead of it, as matching buy quantity arrives. A fill can also occur because orders ahead are cancelled or because the market moves away from the circuit. The displayed aggregate queue does not show the user's exact queue rank, cancellations, or future incoming buyers.

The statement “sell immediately at 09:00” also needs correction. The screenshots themselves show pre-open indications, and the exchange uses an opening-price discovery process before continuous trading. An early order may improve time priority, but it does not create buyers and does not guarantee a fill. Unmatched orders can carry into continuous trading while retaining timestamps under the applicable exchange process.

Therefore the model must estimate a distribution of **time-to-fill and locked-circuit duration**, including a no-fill branch. It must never hard-code “one-hour exit” or “maximum 10% loss.” The exact third-cycle broker order log is required before treating that event as calibrated evidence.

### Proposed prospective decision states

1. **Observation only:** candidate detected; no order. Record all features and the hypothetical fill constraint.
2. **Liquidity confirmation:** rely on executed trade flow and repeated queue snapshots, not a single displayed imbalance.
3. **Entry feasibility:** mark whether an order could actually have filled without hindsight.
4. **Exit feasibility:** simulate the user's order behind the visible same-price sell queue and record partial fills and time-to-fill.
5. **Failure state:** consecutive lower circuits, suspension/surveillance restriction, or no-fill by the predefined deadline.

Any eventual signal threshold must be derived from prospective data. A 20% take-profit may be tested as one rule, but it should not be assumed optimal merely because the observed rallies later reached 30–40%.

## Limitations

The screenshots span different times, phases, and applications; some figures may be stale or pre-open. They cannot reveal who placed/cancelled orders or whether accounts are connected. No finding here accuses any named company or trader of manipulation. This is research and risk education, not personalized investment advice or a recommendation to buy, hold, or sell any security.
